Approach A · Detailed implementation plan

Production line monitoring, built for the factory floor.

A dedicated mobile workflow for نموذج مراقبة خط الإنتاج. QC completes one record for each hourly inspection. Each modification is saved to Convex while connected, product-specific metric units and business limits come from the web Form Settings, the five process measurements accept multiple readings, every record receives a hall-prefixed serial number, and production management reviews and co-signs on web.

Replace one active paper workflow end to end

The first release digitizes the production-line monitoring form without building a generic form builder. The QC organization enters the record in the Expo mobile app during production. One form represents one inspection time, performed every hour across the factory's 24/7 operation. Each modification is saved through a connected Convex mutation. The production manager reviews the submitted record and adds the second signature from the existing web workspace.

In scope

  • Arabic-first, RTL mobile entry with English translations available.
  • Connected Convex autosave after every committed field modification.
  • Repeatable readings for pour weight, additive weight, chocolate temperature, coated-piece weight, and carton weight.
  • Single carton-count and cleanliness-percentage values, 22 conformity checks, notes, and two signatures.

  • One required photo for every non-conforming check, uploaded from iOS or Android.
  • QC submit, web production return-for-correction, web production approve, and approved lock.
  • Web review queue, record detail, audit history, search, and PDF export.
  • Hourly inspection identity and duplicate prevention for the same line, product, and hour.
  • Server-assigned form serial numbers such as A 1126 and B 1233, where the letter identifies the production hall and the number is a non-reused counting sequence.

  • Role checks, workspace isolation, immutable approval events, and indefinite retention until a policy is approved.

Out of scope for this release

  • A generic form-template designer or generic mobile form renderer.
  • The laboratory, receiving, kitchen, kitchen-control, and raw-material forms.
  • Automatic machine or sensor ingestion.
  • Local draft persistence and deferred saving without a backend connection.
  • Claiming FDA, ISO 22000, HACCP, or local legal certification.
  • Replacing paper before the company completes a controlled parallel pilot.
  • Production-manager review or approval from mobile.
  • Password or biometric re-entry when QC signs.
  • Voiding, amending, or automated retention deletion before the company defines an ownership and retention policy.

02 · What was agreed

Premises and operating signals

Premises

  1. QC completes the form in the mobile app while production is running.
  2. Production management reviews the submitted record.
  3. QC and production both sign the same record revision.
  4. Each committed modification is saved directly to Convex.
  5. The five named process measurements may each contain multiple readings.
  6. The current phase requires an active connection for editing and submission.
  7. One form represents one inspection time and a new form is due every hour, continuously.
  8. Each form has a hall-prefixed serial identifier. The serial identifies a form instance and is separate from product-specification version numbers.
  9. This plan assumes Convex allocates the next serial. If the factory uses pre-numbered physical forms that QC must transcribe, the creation workflow must change before implementation.
  10. The QC inspector photographs the printed carton label for رقم الدفعة.
  11. This plan assumes QC manually confirms the printed batch code against the required photo; OCR is not authoritative in the first release.
  12. Metric units and business limits vary by product and are supplied by an authorized user through the web Form Settings.
  13. Every non-conformity requires at least one photo before QC can submit.
  14. Approved records cannot be silently edited or deleted.

Signals

  • The company already uses a concrete paper form, which is stronger evidence than a hypothetical request.
  • The responsible people and approval handoff are named.
  • The required device and moment of entry are named: mobile during production.
  • The current scope requires connectivity and does not include local persistence.
  • The current mobile app has shared Convex and Better Auth wiring but no domain workflow yet.

03 · Selected approach

Dedicated production-line form

Decision: build a typed form and workflow for one production use case. Keep the storage boundaries clean enough to extract shared primitives after the organization has observed real mobile usage.

Why this path

  • It gets one active workflow into factory use faster.
  • Fixed fields support stronger validation and a clearer mobile layout.
  • The fixed model can represent repeated readings without introducing arbitrary form schemas.
  • Observed use will reveal which primitives are actually shared by the later forms.

Delivery and risk

Delivery: five implementation phases followed by a controlled pilot.

Risk: medium. The highest risks are network availability, concurrent edits, signature meaning, and operational adoption.

Rejected alternatives

Configurable compliance-form engine

Rejected for the first release because arbitrary schemas, repeatable sections, migrations, and generic rendering would delay the first factory form and increase the test surface before mobile behavior is observed.

Production-session event stream

Rejected because it changes the familiar operating model. It may be a better later UX, but the first pilot should preserve the recognizable form and approval process.

04 · Operational workflow

One record, four controlled states

Editable Draft QC enters data. Every committed change saves directly to Convex.
Review Production review QC signs online. The signed revision is frozen while production reviews it.
Editable Returned QC corrects the record. The old signed revision remains in the audit history.
Locked Approved Production co-signs. Direct edits and deletion are blocked.

Step-by-step usage

  1. QC signs in and opens the Quality tab in the mobile app.
  2. QC selects production hall A or B, department or line, and product for the current hourly inspection slot.
  3. A connected Convex mutation creates one draft for that line, product, and hour, then atomically assigns the next non-reused serial for the selected hall.
  4. The active web Form Settings version, product name, metric units, and business limits are snapshotted on the record.
  5. QC photographs the printed carton label, enters the visible code such as 060826 1, and confirms that the image and entered code match.
  6. QC adds one or more readings for each repeatable measurement and completes all conformity checks.
  7. Each reading is evaluated against the snapshotted product limit. Out-of-range readings are clearly marked.
  8. Any non-conforming check requires a note, immediate action, and at least one uploaded photo before submission.
  9. The app displays “Saving,” “Saved,” or “Save failed” independently from form validation.
  10. QC reviews a summary and taps “Sign and submit.” The current authenticated session is sufficient; no password or biometric prompt is added.
  11. The production manager opens the web review queue, checks data and deviations, then approves or returns the record with a required reason.
  12. Approval creates the second signature event, locks the revision, and makes the official PDF available.
Connection boundary: editing and submission require connectivity in this phase. The UI must not show “Saved” until the backend acknowledges the mutation, and it must block submission while any change has failed to save.

Required non-conformity evidence

  • Selecting non_compliant immediately reveals note, immediate-action, and photo controls.
  • At least one successfully uploaded photo is required for each non-compliant check.
  • The pilot allows up to three photos per check, subject to confirmation with the quality organization.
  • The app compresses images before upload and stores file metadata separately from the check.
  • Changing a check back to compliant does not silently delete evidence. The draft asks for confirmation, then removes the attachment through an audited server mutation.

06 · Mobile product shape

Fast entry without reproducing a wide paper table

Navigation

  • Add a Quality tab to the authenticated Expo Router tab group.
  • Show the current organization-local hourly inspection slot when QC creates a record.
  • Show “New,” “Drafts,” “Submitted,” and “Returned” filters.
  • Open the form as a stack screen so the tab bar does not compete with entry actions.
  • Display a persistent connectivity and save-state bar at the top.

Form layout

  • Step 1: production hall, read-only form serial, hourly inspection time, department or line, product, carton batch-label capture, printed-code confirmation, and optional run.
  • Step 2: active product specification plus five measurement groups, each with an “Add reading” action and ordered reading rows.
  • Step 3: conformity checks grouped into four sections.
  • Step 4: deviations, required photos, notes, completion summary, and QC signature.
  • Sticky “Previous” and “Next” controls, with no disabled fields hidden from the user.

Interaction rules

  • Use numeric keyboards for weights, temperature, percentage, and carton count.
  • Keep measurement units visible beside inputs. Do not encode units only in placeholder text.
  • Show the configured acceptable range beside each repeatable measurement and mark every out-of-range reading.
  • Use large segmented controls for compliant, non-compliant, and not applicable.
  • Move focus to the deviation note when “non-compliant” is selected.
  • Open camera or photo-library capture from the non-conforming check and show upload progress.
  • Use camera capture only for رقم الدفعة, show the captured carton label beside the printed-code field, and require an explicit match confirmation.
  • Keep the server-assigned form serial visible in the header and read-only throughout the workflow.
  • Show completion by section, not one vague total percentage.
  • Save each committed modification through a Convex mutation.
  • Save selectors and conformity choices immediately. Debounce free-text fields, then flush them on blur, navigation, and submit review.
  • Serialize saves per record so a slower response cannot replace a newer value.
  • Support Arabic RTL and English LTR using the same fixed field keys.
  • Verify the same flow on supported iOS and Android phones.
  • Do not put raw form values in Bugsink breadcrumbs or analytics events.

07 · Connected autosave

Granular Convex mutations after each modification

Mobile form

Typed state, local validation, optimistic UI, and a visible save status.

→

Save coordinator

Debounces text, serializes writes per record, and tracks the last acknowledged revision.

→

Convex

Authoritative drafts, readings, checks, revisions, signatures, events, and web review.

Save behavior

  1. Create the draft online before opening editable fields.
  2. Apply structural validation in the mobile form.
  3. For selectors, toggles, reading additions, reading edits, and reading removals, call the matching mutation immediately.
  4. For notes and other free text, debounce saves for 500 milliseconds and flush on blur or navigation.
  5. Keep one serialized mutation chain per record so saves are applied in user order.
  6. Show “Saved” only after the latest requested revision is acknowledged.
  7. On failure, keep the value visible in memory, mark it unsaved, provide retry, and block signing.

Reading operations

Each repeatable reading is its own child record. Adding a reading creates a new row with a server ID. Editing changes only that row. Removing a reading deletes it only while the parent record is editable. Reordering is optional in the first phase; display order may use observation time and creation order.

Concurrent edit boundary

The pilot supports one editable owner and one primary device per draft. A second device can view the record but cannot edit it. An explicit “Take over editing” action may be added after pilot feedback. This rule removes most multi-device ordering problems.

  • Each save includes the last acknowledged recordRevision.
  • The server rejects a stale revision and returns the current record state.
  • The mobile app stops further saves and asks the user to reload rather than overwriting a newer server value.
  • Submitted and approved revisions remain protected by workflow-state checks.
Connection boundary: if connectivity drops, the app marks the form unavailable for further edits until the backend connection returns.

Data invariants

  • The draft is created in Convex before editable fields are shown.
  • Draft creation atomically allocates the next serial for the selected production hall; the display value is the hall code, one space, and the sequence.
  • Serial numbers are unique within organization and hall and are never reused, including after a draft is discarded.
  • Only one record may exist for the same organization, department or line, product, and organization-local hourly slot.
  • A record snapshots one active product specification version when it is created.
  • Only draft and returned records accept autosave mutations.
  • A record cannot enter production review until all required fields and checks are valid.
  • Each reading uses the snapshotted unit and is evaluated against the snapshotted limits.
  • Each reading key meets its configured minimum reading count.
  • Carton count and cleanliness percentage remain single record-level values.
  • Submission requires one captured carton-label photo, a valid QC-confirmed printed code, its parsed production date, and a daily batch sequence from 1–9.
  • The serial number identifies the form record; specificationVersion identifies the business-limit version. They must never be presented as the same value.
  • Every non-compliant check has a deviation note, immediate action, and at least one uploaded photo.
  • QC and production signatures must reference the exact record revision they signed.
  • Approval fails when the current revision differs from the QC-signed revision.
  • Approved records cannot be patched or deleted by client-facing mutations.
  • Approved records are retained indefinitely until the company adopts a retention and correction policy.

Permissions

  • view_production_checks: read approved or assigned records.
  • manage_production_specifications: create and publish product metric units and business limits in the web Form Settings.

Add a quality_officer system role with record/view permissions and a production_manager role with review/view permissions. Do not automatically reassign existing users. Workspace admins assign roles explicitly. Existing admin and supervisor behavior must be reviewed during migration so current access is not reduced.

Convex implementation constraints

  • Read packages/backend/convex/_generated/ai/guidelines.md before editing backend files.
  • Define every table in schema.ts and every shared validator in validators.ts.
  • Keep function argument validators, return validators, and handlers synchronized.
  • Resolve the authenticated user on the server. Never accept signer or actor identity from the client.
  • Validate workspace membership for every read and mutation.
  • Use indexes for review filters. Do not collect all workspace records and filter in memory.
  • Allocate the serial counter and create the record in one mutation. Check the hourly-slot identity before incrementing so a retry returns the existing record without consuming another serial.
  • Validate hall code server-side and never accept a client-supplied serial sequence.
  • Parse batch-label dates with an explicit DDMMYY rule and real calendar validation; do not rely on locale-dependent date parsing.
  • Keep child reads bounded. Set a documented maximum number of readings per measurement key for the first release.
  • Write the record, children, specification snapshot, signature, and audit event in one mutation when a workflow transition requires atomicity.
  • Do not expose void, amendment, or delete mutations for approved records until the company adopts a policy.

10 · Quality Control workspace

Production review, record search, and official output

Routes

  • /quality/production-line-records: queue and searchable history.
  • /quality/production-line-records/:id: review and record detail.
  • /settings, Production Line Form Settings section: versioned per-product metric units and business limits.

Web Form Settings: Production Line Specifications

  • Restrict editing and publishing to manage_production_specifications.
  • Select a product, view the active version, and create a new draft version.
  • Configure a metric unit, minimum, maximum, optional target, and minimum reading count for all five reading keys.
  • Limit weight units to mg, g, and kg; use °C for chocolate temperature.
  • Validate that every maximum is at least its minimum and every optional target falls inside the range.
  • Preview how readings will appear in mobile before publishing.
  • Publishing makes the version immutable and active for future hourly forms.
  • Show which approved records use each specification version. Never recalculate historical records against new limits.

Web Form Settings: Production halls and serial counters

  • Configure the enabled hall codes as A and B and show the last allocated serial for each hall.
  • Before the first digital form is created, an authorized administrator seeds the next sequence from the corresponding paper-form counter.
  • After allocation begins, the next sequence is server-managed and read-only in normal settings. Correcting it requires a separately audited administrative procedure.
  • Display serial examples as A 1126 and B 1233. Do not call the product specification version a form serial.

Review queue

  • Default to records waiting for production review.
  • Show form serial, production hall, department, product, printed batch code, QC signer, submitted time, non-conformity count, and reading completeness.
  • Filter by status, date range, hall, form serial, product, printed batch code, department, QC user, and production reviewer.
  • Sort review work oldest first. Mark records with non-conformities clearly.
  • Use paginated Convex queries from the start.

Review detail

  • Render the same logical sections as mobile, optimized for desktop reading.
  • Show the five reading groups in compact tables with value, unit, observer, and observation time.
  • Separate non-conforming checks at the top while preserving the complete checklist below.
  • Show QC signature identity, signed revision, client time, and server time.
  • Require a reason when returning a record.
  • Require production-manager confirmation before approval. Approval must fail if the record revision changed.
  • Show the active specification snapshot and audit history in chronological order.
  • Show the captured carton label beside its QC-confirmed printed batch code, parsed production date, and daily batch sequence.
  • Show required non-conformity photos beside their checks.

PDF output

  • Generate an Arabic-capable official record with the company form title and familiar table groupings.
  • Include form serial, record ID, workspace, hall, product, department, printed batch code, parsed label date and sequence, carton-label image, inspection dates, all repeatable readings, all checks, notes, and both signature receipts.
  • Until grouped sampling is confirmed, render each repeatable measurement as its own numbered list with observation times. Do not align unrelated readings into artificial rows.
  • Include revision and snapshot hash in the footer.
  • Do not regenerate an old approved PDF from current mutable profile data. Use signature and name snapshots stored at signing time.

11 · Security and record integrity

Authentication is necessary; immutable events make signatures meaningful

Mobile device

  • Keep Better Auth tokens in Expo SecureStore, as the current app does.
  • Stop autosave and switch the form to a connection-required state when Convex is unavailable.
  • Clear in-memory form state during sign-out and reload the authoritative record after sign-in.
  • Do not log field values, notes, product secrets, or signatures to Bugsink.

Server

  • Derive actor identity and permissions on every mutation.
  • Store server timestamps for authoritative workflow events.
  • Keep client observation times for production context, but do not trust them for authorization.
  • Reject content updates to submitted, approved, or wrong-workspace records.
  • Keep approved snapshots, signatures, events, specification snapshots, carton-label images, and non-conformity photos immutable.

Signature policy

A drawn or uploaded signature image is presentation data. The authoritative signature receipt contains the authenticated user ID, name and role snapshot, signature purpose, signed revision, server timestamp, and immutable snapshot reference. The server creates the signature only after revalidating auth, permission, record state, content revision, and the absence of failed or pending autosaves. The current authenticated QC session is sufficient; the first release does not request a password or device biometric again.

Photo access and validation

  • Authorize every upload attachment and download against the record workspace and the caller's role.
  • Accept only configured image MIME types and size limits, compress on mobile, and store file metadata with its declared batch-label or non-conformity purpose.
  • Do not expose permanent public file URLs. Resolve photos through authorized backend queries.
  • Allow photo removal only while the draft is editable, and record the removal event.

Deletion and correction

  • A server draft is discarded through an authorized mutation that records the event.
  • A submitted record is returned, not reopened silently.
  • An approved record is retained indefinitely in the first release and cannot be edited, voided, amended, or deleted.
  • Operational errors in approved records are escalated outside the app during the pilot until ownership and correction policy are approved.
Regulatory boundary: this design follows common record-integrity controls, but retention periods, signature wording, device controls, and whether specific regulations apply must be approved by the company's quality and legal owners before paper is retired.

13 · Verification

Test the failure paths before the happy-path pilot

Autosave coordinator tests

  • Every committed selector, toggle, and reading change calls its mutation.
  • Rapid text edits debounce to the last value and flush on blur.
  • Per-record serialization prevents an older response from replacing a newer modification.
  • Save failure marks the field or form as unsaved and blocks submission.
  • Manual retry writes the latest visible value, not a stale captured value.
  • Expired auth requests sign-in and does not claim that the change was saved.
  • A stale record revision stops further edits until the server copy is reloaded.

Backend authorization tests

  • QC can update only editable records assigned to that user and workspace.
  • Production reviewers cannot edit field content.
  • Users without review permission cannot approve or return a record.
  • Cross-workspace reads and writes fail.
  • Approval fails if the QC-signed revision is no longer current.
  • Approved records reject patch and delete operations.
  • Only authorized web Form Settings users can draft and publish product specifications.
  • Only manage_production_line_settings can seed a hall counter, and initialization fails after that hall allocates its first digital serial.
  • A second record for the same workspace, line, product, and local hourly slot is rejected.
  • Concurrent creation of different hourly drafts in the same hall produces distinct increasing serial sequences.
  • A retry after a committed draft creation returns the existing hourly record without allocating another serial.
  • Hall A and hall B records receive the correct prefix and cannot collide inside their hall sequence.
  • Batch-label parsing rejects invalid calendar dates, missing date digits, missing separators, sequence 0, and multi-digit sequences in the confirmed pilot format.
  • Submission fails without both the carton-label photo and QC-confirmed printed batch code.
  • Submission fails when a non-conforming check has no attached photo.

Mobile behavior tests

  • Arabic RTL order, labels, numeric fields, and validation render correctly on Android and iOS.
  • All controls meet practical touch-target size for factory use.
  • Each of the five measurement groups can add, edit, and remove multiple readings.
  • Each measurement displays the configured unit and acceptable range, and marks an out-of-range reading immediately after server acknowledgement.
  • Carton count and cleanliness percentage remain single-value inputs.
  • The form serial is read-only and remains stable after reload, return, and resubmission.
  • The carton-label field opens the device camera, not the photo library, and requires the printed code to be confirmed against the captured image.
  • 060826 1 is normalized, parsed as 2026-08-06 and daily batch 1, then restored identically after reload.
  • Connection loss switches the screen to a clear connection-required state.
  • The submit action remains blocked while a save is pending or failed.
  • Non-compliant checks require an explanation, corrective action, and at least one photo.
  • Camera and photo-library capture, compression, upload progress, retry, preview, and draft removal work on supported iOS and Android phones.

Web and document tests

  • Review queue filters and pagination return only the active workspace.
  • Web Form Settings rejects non-metric units and validates every fixed measurement key before publishing a specification version.
  • Publishing a new version changes future hourly forms but does not alter existing record snapshots.
  • Return requires a reason and reopens only the correct QC workflow.
  • Production-manager review and approval work on web without requiring a mobile review screen.
  • The queue can search by form serial and printed batch code.
  • The review and PDF show the same immutable carton-label image and parsed code that QC signed.
  • Approval locks the record and writes the production signature once.
  • The review and PDF show authorized non-conformity photos from the approved snapshot.
  • Arabic PDF text, tables, signatures, and page breaks render correctly.
  • PDF content continues to match the approved snapshot after profile names or signatures change.

Operational pilot scenarios

  • Enter several readings in each measurement group, reload the record, and compare every value and time.
  • Seed hall A to sequence 1126, create a form, and verify the server assigns A 1126 exactly once.
  • Create records in halls A and B and verify each hall uses its own configured counter assumption.
  • Capture a carton label showing 060826 1, confirm the transcription, reload, and compare the image and parsed fields.
  • Capture a later label for the same date with daily sequence 2 and verify it is searchable as a separate mix batch.
  • Attempt to create two forms for the same line, product, and hour, and verify that the second action opens or reports the existing record.
  • Publish a new product specification and verify that an existing draft keeps its old snapshot while the next hourly form receives the new version.
  • Mark a check non-conforming and verify submission stays blocked until its photo is uploaded and attached.
  • Lose connectivity mid-edit and verify the app reports the unsaved modification without claiming success.
  • Allow auth to expire during entry, reauthenticate, reload, and continue from the last server-confirmed value.
  • Return a signed record, correct it on mobile, resubmit, and verify both signed revisions remain visible.

15 · Release definition

Acceptance criteria

  • An authenticated QC user with an active connection can create a server draft.
  • Each form identifies one inspection time and one organization-local hourly slot; the same workspace, line, product, and slot cannot have two records.
  • Each draft has a server-assigned read-only serial formatted as hall A or B, one space, and a non-reused counting number.
  • Serial allocation is atomic and idempotent: concurrent creation cannot duplicate a serial, and retrying the same hourly draft does not consume another number.
  • An authorized user can configure and publish metric weight units, Celsius temperature, minimum and maximum business limits, optional targets, and minimum reading counts per product in web Form Settings.
  • A record stores the specification version and limit snapshot used when it was created; later settings changes affect only later hourly forms.
  • Every acknowledged modification is present after closing and reopening the mobile app.
  • The app always distinguishes saving, saved, save failed, and connection required.
  • A save failure blocks submission and provides a retry action.
  • Pour weight, additive weight, chocolate temperature, coated-piece weight, and carton weight each accept multiple readings.
  • Each reading stores value, unit, observation time, observer, order, applicable limits, and the computed within-limit result.
  • A complete form includes the required header, the configured minimum readings for each repeatable measurement, carton count, cleanliness percentage, all 22 conformity checks, and required non-conformity details.
  • QC must capture a carton-label photo with the device camera and confirm its printed code before submission.
  • The code 060826 1 is stored in normalized form, parsed as production date 2026-08-06 and daily mix-batch sequence 1, and bound to the signed snapshot.
  • Daily batch sequence accepts 1–9 so a newly prepared mix can advance from 1 to 2 and onward on the same label date within the confirmed one-digit format.
  • Each non-conforming check requires at least one authorized photo before submission.
  • The entry and photo flow passes supported-device checks on both iOS and Android phones.
  • QC submission creates a signature event tied to an immutable revision using the current authenticated session, with no password or biometric re-entry.
  • The production manager can review on web, return with a reason, or approve with the second signature.
  • No user can directly edit or delete an approved record.
  • The first release exposes no void, amend, or approved-record deletion action; approved records remain retained until a formal policy is implemented.
  • The web workspace can search and filter records by inspection date, hall, form serial, product, printed batch code, parsed label date, daily batch sequence, status, and department.
  • The official Arabic PDF matches the approved snapshot and includes the form serial, carton-label evidence, and both signature receipts.
  • Cross-workspace access tests fail closed.
  • The pilot can reconcile every mobile record with its parallel paper record.

16 · Risks and open decisions

Resolve these before paper retirement

RiskEffectMitigation
Product specification is configured incorrectly Hourly readings can be assessed against the wrong unit or acceptable range. Require complete limits for all five keys, validate min/target/max ordering, restrict publishing permission, show a pre-publish summary, and retain immutable versions.
Signature policy is not legally reviewed A digital sign-off may not meet the company's applicable policy or jurisdiction. Store strong signature receipts now. The agreed pilot uses the current authenticated session without reauthentication; obtain quality/legal approval for wording, retention, and device controls before paper retirement.
Retention, void, and amendment ownership is undefined The organization has no approved in-app process for correcting or disposing of approved records. Retain approved records indefinitely and expose no void, amendment, or delete action in the first release. Define the policy before adding those workflows.
Hall serial scope or cutover value is wrong Digital forms can duplicate an existing paper serial or continue the wrong hall's sequence. Confirm whether counters are independent per hall, reconcile the last paper serial for A and B, seed each counter once, allocate server-side atomically, and never reuse an allocated number.
Printed batch code is transcribed incorrectly Search and reconciliation metadata can disagree with the carton-label evidence. Show the image beside the input, parse the date and sequence structurally, require QC match confirmation, preserve the image in the signed snapshot, and expose both to the web reviewer.
Multiple devices edit one draft Concurrent autosaves can overwrite newer values or produce confusing failures. Use one editable owner/device, serialize saves, and reject stale record revisions. Add takeover only after observing the need.
Factory connectivity is unstable QC cannot save or submit during an outage in the current phases. Show connection state before entry, fail visibly, and keep paper as the pilot fallback.
High autosave mutation volume Rapid typing or many readings can create avoidable writes and visible latency. Save discrete controls immediately, debounce text, serialize per record, and measure save latency during the pilot.
Photo upload fails on a weak connection A required carton-label or non-conformity photo can delay hourly submission. Compress images, display upload progress and retry, keep the form editable until attachment succeeds, and retain paper as the pilot fallback.
Paper layout is treated as mobile UX A wide table becomes slow and error-prone on a phone. Use vertical cards and grouped checks while preserving familiar section names and official PDF layout.

Open questions

  1. What is the maximum expected number of readings for each repeatable measurement?
  2. Are the five readings normally captured together at one observation time or independently?
  3. How long must approved records be retained, and who can void or amend them?
  4. Which web Form Settings timezone defines the hourly slot, especially during timezone or daylight-saving changes?
  5. How are active production lines and products assigned so the system can distinguish a missed hourly inspection from an intentionally idle line?
  6. Does the counting sequence run independently for hall A and hall B, or is one number sequence shared across both halls?
  7. What are the next serial numbers for halls A and B when digital entry starts?
  8. Are gaps acceptable when a numbered draft is discarded, provided its serial is never reused?
  9. Can the daily mix-batch sequence exceed one digit, for example 060826 10?
  10. May the carton-label production date differ from the hourly inspection date when production crosses midnight?
  11. Should the digital app allocate each form serial, or must QC transcribe a serial already printed on a controlled paper form?
  12. Should the printed batch code remain manual QC transcription, become OCR-assisted with QC confirmation, or be stored as image-only evidence?
